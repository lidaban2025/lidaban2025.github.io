Google Forms Approval Workflow: How to Route Submissions to Approvers

Google Forms collects answers, but it has no built-in approval routing. Here are the three working ways to send each submission to the right person, collect their decision, and keep an audit trail — with the trade-offs of each.

What you actually need

A usable approval workflow answers four questions for every submission:

  1. Who approves it? — the approver depends on the answers (department, cost center, requester's manager).
  2. How do they act? — ideally a link in an email, not "log into a sheet."
  3. Where is the decision recorded? — back into the responses sheet, with a timestamp.
  4. What happens after? — requester notified, next step triggered.

Option 1: Manual routing from the responses sheet

Link the form to a Google Sheet, then forward rows to approvers by email yourself. Zero setup, fine for a handful of requests per week — but every step is manual: picking the approver, copying the link, chasing decisions, and copying results back. There is no audit trail beyond your outbox.

Option 2: Apps Script (free, DIY)

An onFormSubmit trigger can read the answers, look up the approver in a mapping sheet, send them MailApp.sendEmail() with the response link, and write the decision back when they reply through a second form. It works, and it's free. The costs show up over time: triggers silently fail when quotas are hit, the mapping sheet drifts from the form fields, and every change needs a developer. If you go this route, keep the routing table in one sheet and log every decision row with a timestamp.

Option 3: FormFlow (add-on, no code)

FormFlow is a Google Forms add-on that does the same loop without scripts: you define approval routes in a connected Google Sheet, FormFlow emails the right approver for each submission with a one-click decision link, and the approval decision is written back to the sheet automatically. A queued-sync mechanism retries approvals when triggers are throttled, so decisions don't get lost in busy periods. Setup is one sidebar: pick the sheet, define the routes, save.

Straight answers to common questions: Google Forms has no native approval routing (as of 2026). Google Sheets alone can't send approval emails. And if someone tells you "just use e-signature software," that solves signing, not routing — most leave-request and purchase-request cases just need the right person to click approve and have it recorded.

Try the no-code route: install FormFlow from the Workspace Marketplace, open its sidebar in your form, and point it at a sheet with one route as a test.
